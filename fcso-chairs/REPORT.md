## Short answer

The SPEC's expected outcome (FCSO whole at 70 %, 2-4× slower than GD) is only partly reproduced.

- **Time.** On the chairs both methods brought to the threshold, FCSO took a median **3.9× GD's time to 50 %**
  (9 chairs, per-chair range 1.0-14.2×) and **5.3× to 70 %** (6 chairs, 1.5-27.5×). Unpaired medians to 70 %:
  254 s vs 76 s (3.3×). One FCSO cycle costs 2.0× a GD cycle (15.9 s vs 8.1 s). The rest of the overhead is extra
  cycles (2.6× more gradient steps to 70 %), because each correction gives back part of Phase 1's progress.
- **Success.** At 50 % / 70 %: GD 10/10 and 10/10; FCSO 9/10 and **6/10**. The 4 FCSO failures are budget stalls at
  final V/V₀ 0.36-0.69, with the chair still whole.
- **Shape.** GD shatters every chair: at 70 %, 0/10 whole, a mean of 55.6 stray components holding 29.1 % of the
  volume. FCSO has fewer strays on all 6 chairs where both methods reach 70 %, and a smaller stray volume on 5 of
  them (median stray share 1.2 % vs 42.4 % for GD). But only 2 of its 6 70 % chairs have no stray component at all,
  and by eye only **3 of the 6 are still recognisable** (2b5953c9, 7824e41c, 57dfb295, all thinned smoothly). FCSO
  shreds the mesh-back office chair 541746dd like GD does (49 % stray volume), and it turns the two bulky upholstered
  chairs (ce95fa90, 447856e1) into off-manifold blocks (Chamfer to the initial 0.56 and 0.61, vs 0.11 and 0.23 for
  GD). The stray-component count under-reports this.
- **Why.** FCSO behaves in two regimes, depending on how large the volume gradient is compared with the ODE drift.
  On the thin chairs the guidance term is small (|g|/|d| ≈ 0.1 at the first ODE step). There the correction adds
  back about +12 % of V each cycle, which cancels a 15-step Adam phase once the chair is thin, and the run stalls
  with a whole chair. On the bulky chairs the same λ makes the guidance 2-13× the drift. It pins V, but it acts as
  an unprojected volume gradient and pushes the latent off the prior (|z − z_s| = 2352 on ce95fa90, against ≈ 60
  elsewhere, with |z_s| ≈ 64).

## Discussion

### Time overhead

FCSO pays twice. A cycle costs 2.0× a GD cycle: Phase 2 runs 10 denoiser forwards and 10 guidance backwards through
the denoiser and the decoder, ≈ 7.5 s on top of Phase 1's ≈ 8 s. It also needs more cycles, because Phase 2 undoes
part of Phase 1. The median paired ratio grows with the threshold (2.0-2.2× at 10-30 %, 3.9× at 50 %, 5.3× at 70 %),
because the correction pulls back harder the thinner the chair gets. At 50 % this is inside the paper's 2-4×
(Table 4); at 70 % it is above. The 70 % ratio also leaves out the 4 chairs where FCSO never got there: each spent
≈ 1060 s at the budget, while GD reached 70 % on them in 41-89 s. The per-chair spread is large (1.0× on 541746dd
at 50 %, 27.5× on 7824e41c at 70 %).

### Shape quality

What the renders show (`report/grid_70.png`, viewer):

- **GD (λ_reg 0)**: every chair breaks before 70 %. Thin members (legs, backrest frames, slats, mesh backs) turn into
  clouds of red fragments; the office chairs lose their base or backrest; the cantilever's seat splits off the
  frame. 0/10 whole at 50 % and at 70 %.
- **GD (λ_reg 1e-3)**: the same drift, milder on some chairs (20b0a820, d409dbda and 7824e41c keep a frame). The
  regularizer stops f215c608 and bbba0832 at 0.80 and 0.78 (budget).
- **FCSO (x0, λ 1e4)** reaches 70 % on 6 chairs. Three of them are thinned versions of the same chair, which is the
  result the paper describes: 2b5953c9 (1 component, thin shells), 7824e41c (1 component, slatted backrest) and
  57dfb295 (slatted backrest, 22 specks holding 1.3 % of the volume). The other three are not: 541746dd breaks like
  GD (49 % stray volume), ce95fa90 becomes a block with a floating bar, and 447856e1 a box with no legs. The 4
  budget-stopped chairs (20b0a820, f215c608, d409dbda, bbba0832) stay whole and recognisable, at final V/V₀
  0.36-0.69.
- Stray components alone flatter FCSO on the bulky chairs: 447856e1 at 70 % has only 1 % stray volume, but a
  Chamfer of 0.61 to the initial chair (GD: 0.23). Mean Chamfer at 70 % is 0.252 for FCSO vs 0.087 for GD: when
  FCSO goes wrong, it ends further from the original shape than GD.

### The stall mechanism

Take the last 20 cycles of the 4 budget-stopped FCSO runs. Phase 1 multiplies V by 0.70-0.89 and the correction
multiplies it by 1.12-1.35 (medians: 20b0a820 ×0.879 then ×1.130; d409dbda ×0.889 / ×1.118; f215c608 ×0.891 /
×1.119; bbba0832 ×0.704 / ×1.345). The two cancel, so r oscillates in a narrow band (e.g. 0.63-0.66 on f215c608)
until the budget runs out. The correction pulls the latent back toward a typical chair, and the pull grows as the
chair gets thinner than the prior's typical thickness. Milestone 3 already measured +9.5 % (f215c608) and +15 %
(57dfb295) on the unthinned chairs. Over all cycles of all chairs, the median V after Phase 2 / c is 1.117 with x0
guidance and 1.135 with no guidance: on these chairs the calibrated guidance removes only a small part of the
wash-out.

The second regime is the chairs where the volume gradient through the denoiser is large. On 447856e1, 541746dd,
57dfb295 and ce95fa90, the same λ makes the guidance term 1.9-12.8× the ODE drift at the first step (0.09-0.16 on
the stalled chairs). There, V after Phase 2 equals c (median 1.00 on 447856e1 and ce95fa90) and progress is fast
(105-630 steps to 70 %). But the guidance then acts as a second, unprojected volume gradient, and that is what
pushes the chair off the manifold: |z − z_s| reaches 2352 on ce95fa90, 404 on 447856e1 and 122 on 541746dd, against
35-78 on the chairs that stay whole. Milestone 6 saw the same effect with noisy-state λ × 3.

### 64³ estimator vs 128³ mesh

Thresholds and V/V₀ are measured on the 64³ estimator, which is the quantity being optimized (SPEC). On average the
128³ mesh ratio agrees (median gap ≤ 0.02 for every method), but not chair by chair. Mean |mesh − V/V₀|: GD 0.094,
FCSO 0.087, GD λ_reg 0.063, FCSO noisy 0.065, FCSO without guidance 0.027, FMG 0.032. The gap goes both ways:

- On thin chairs, the optimizer thins members until they fall between 64³ grid points, so the 64³ volume drops
  faster than the real one. GD bbba0832 at "70 %" is 0.28 at 64³ but 0.66 at 128³; GD d409dbda 0.22 vs 0.47. FCSO
  does it too: bbba0832 at "50 %" is 0.49 vs 0.96, f215c608 final 0.64 vs 0.84, 57dfb295 at 70 % 0.28 vs 0.41.
- On shattered shapes, the 64³ sigmoid sum over-counts thin sheets and dust: GD 541746dd 0.27 vs 0.15, FCSO noisy
  ce95fa90 0.25 vs 0.09.

The runs that move least (no guidance, FMG) show the smallest gap. Part of both methods' measured progress on thin
chairs is therefore grid aliasing. A finer optimization grid was not tried: that would be tuning.

### What the optional runs show

- **FCSO without guidance (λ = 0)** shows what guidance is for. The unguided correction cancels Phase 1 on 9/10
  chairs (late cycles: Phase 1 ×0.58-0.98, correction ×1.02-1.68, V after correction / c median 1.135). It succeeds
  on 2/10 chairs at 50 % and 1/10 at 70 % (2b5953c9 only, 750 steps, 450 s), with final V/V₀ 0.48-1.04: 57dfb295
  ends *above* its initial volume. Its shapes are the cleanest of all runs: whole on 6/9 budget stops, ≤ 0.4 % stray
  volume on the others, and the smallest 64³/128³ gap. Guidance (x0, λ 1e4) raises 70 % success from 1/10 to 6/10,
  but mostly on the chairs where it dominates the drift, and those are the chairs it breaks. As in the paper's
  Appendix E, removing guidance washes out progress. Unlike in the paper, the guided version does not keep both
  shape and progress on every chair.
- **FCSO with the SPEC default (noisy-state guidance, λ 1e3)**: 5/10 at 50 %, 4/10 at 70 %. When it works it is
  faster than x0 (median 128 s to 70 %), but 3 of its 4 70 % chairs are broken (7824e41c 58 % stray volume, ce95fa90
  62 %, 447856e1 a blob); only 2b5953c9 is whole (473 s). This supports keeping x0 as the headline FCSO.
- **FMG (x0, λ 1e4, c = 0.97 V)** is not fast here. A repetition costs 7.8 s, about the price of a GD cycle, but
  the correction lands at V / c ≈ 1.027 (median over all repetitions), so V moves by only about 0.4 % per
  repetition. FMG reaches 70 % on 2/10 chairs, both bulky (ce95fa90: 800 steps, 599 s; 447856e1: 410 steps, 309 s),
  and both times by leaving the original shape: ce95fa90 turns into a spindle-back chair (Chamfer 0.17), 447856e1
  into a blob (Chamfer 0.51). The cantilever chair ends at V/V₀ 1.31. The paper's FMG is driven by a
  volume-conditioned flow with CFG; Option B on a prior that knows nothing about volume gives FMG almost no grip.

### Hypotheses for the deviations from the paper

1. **Prior: class-conditional EDM diffusion instead of a volume-conditioned rectified flow.** The paper's prior knows
   the volume target (CFG on V), so its correction projects onto "chairs of volume c". Ours projects onto "chairs",
   and a typical chair is thicker than one reduced by 50-70 %. Option B can only push on V through one gradient per
   ODE step. The SPEC's σ_start = τ/(1−τ) equivalence holds in distribution, but the two priors do not pull toward
   the same place.
2. **λ: one global value does not fit all these chairs.** |∇V| through the denoiser varies by more than an order of
   magnitude between thin and bulky chairs. The λ calibrated on the 3 thin debug chairs gives |g|/|d| ≈ 0.1 on thin
   chairs, too weak to hold V, and 2-13 on bulky ones, strong enough to leave the manifold. The SPEC's rule sets λ
   from the first ODE step only. A per-chair or per-step normalization (e.g. |g| = k|d|) would be the natural next
   experiment, but it is tuning and was not done.
3. **σ_start = 0.25 is large for thin members.** The unguided correction alone thickens thin-frame chairs by +9.5 %
   and +15 % (milestone 3), and the effect grows smoothly with σ (+1.2 % at 0.05). The thinner the chair gets, the
   stronger the pull back.
4. **512 × 8 class-conditional latents.** The public model covers 55 categories, with a posterior std of ≈ 0.1-0.2
   per entry. The paper's 256 × 8 single-category prior may be tighter, and its correction gentler.
5. **Phase 1 with Adam (lr 0.005) instead of literal SGD.** Adam was needed to move at all (DECISIONS.md). But an
   Adam phase moves each latent coordinate by a fixed amount whatever the gradient scale, so the correction has more
   to undo than after the paper's small SGD steps.
6. **Occupancy logits on a 64³ grid instead of an SDF.** Thin members alias at 64³ (see above), so the optimizer can
   lower the measured volume by making members thinner than a grid cell. An SDF-based estimator would not reward
   this in the same way.

## Exceptions carried from earlier milestones

- **Milestone 3 (prior), criterion met on 8/10 chairs only.** The unguided correction at σ_start = 0.25 should change
  volume by less than 5 %; it adds +9.5 % on the thin dining chair f215c608 and +15.0 % on the lawn chair 57dfb295
  (mean of 3 noise seeds; the other 8 chairs stay within ±3.6 %). Diagnosis (runs/m3/diag.json): not a latent-scale,
  σ-convention, solver or class-label bug; the prior thickens thin members, and the effect grows smoothly with σ_start
  (+1.2 % at 0.05, +3.8 % at 0.1). The user chose to keep σ_start = 0.25 (Table 6) and let Option B guidance counter
  it, rather than tune it away. Milestone 7 shows the cost of that choice: the stall on thin chairs.
- **Milestone 6 (FCSO), criterion not met.** No SPEC-sanctioned setting (noisy λ 1e3, ×3, ÷3; x0 λ 1e4, ×3; Heun)
  reached 70 % with whole chairs on 2 of the 3 debug chairs. Every non-×3 run kept the chairs in one piece, but on
  2/3 chairs the correction gave back each cycle what Phase 1 had gained and the run stalled on the budget; the ×3
  settings reached 70 % more often but by shredding the chair like GD (noisy λ 3e3) or splitting it in three
  (x0 λ 3e4 on the office chair). Nothing was tuned further (SPEC "Expected outcome"); the λ sweep is in
  `runs/m6/summary.md` and is not part of the comparison above. The 10-chair comparison confirms the milestone 6
  picture and adds a second failure mode: bulky chairs leaving the manifold.

## Summary of DECISIONS.md

The full list with rationale is in [`DECISIONS.md`](DECISIONS.md). These are the decisions that shape the results:

- **Data and encoding.** Only the chair folder, fetched by HTTP range requests. Chairs from the test split (the AE
  and the prior were trained on `train`). The per-shape `scale` is applied as in `util/shapenet.py`; 2048 seeded
  surface points; the posterior **mean** is z_s; no latent rescaling (the prior was trained on unscaled posterior
  samples, `sigma_data` = 1).
- **Mac port.** A pure-PyTorch FPS replaces `torch_cluster` via `sys.modules` (third_party untouched); a float32
  port of `edm_sampler` (MPS has no float64); all noise is drawn on the CPU with seeded generators; no op fell back
  to the CPU.
- **Correction.** ρ = 7 EDM schedule with σ_max := σ_start = 0.25, 10 Euler steps (Heun was checked: same result at
  1.9× the cost); ε is redrawn every cycle with seed `seed·100003 + k`.
- **Volume estimator.** Cell-centred 64³ grid (the vertex grid is biased by −4.6 %); β = 1; 48³ rejected (it differs
  from 64³ by up to 17 %); chunked backward with 32k-query chunks; chairs not batched (slower and more memory).
- **Optimizer.** Adam, lr 0.005, state reset every 15-step cycle, for every method. Literal SGD at η = 0.005 would
  have needed ≈ 40-60k steps to 70 %, because our V is ≈ 0.1-0.3, not the paper's 4.5, so the gradient scale
  differs.
- **Budget and snapshots.** 1000 Phase-1 gradient steps (FMG counts its guidance gradients instead); r checked after
  every cycle; the first crossing of each threshold is saved; wall time counts optimization work only;
  budget-stopped runs get a "final" column.
- **Guidance.** Option B, λ = 1e3 in noisy mode and 1e4 in x0 mode, calibrated by the SPEC rule on the 3 debug
  chairs; the sign test passes. The **x0 variant is the headline FCSO**: an allowed SPEC variant, with the same
  quality as noisy on the debug chairs and 3.3× faster on the one debug chair both brought to 70 %. The ×3 settings
  were rejected because they broke chairs.
- **Evaluation.** skimage marching cubes at 128³, every component kept; stray = every component but the largest by
  volume; Chamfer with 10k points (floor ≈ 0.025); fixed orthographic 3/4 camera, white background.
- **Report.** The evaluation saves its meshes (`meshes.npz`), so the report never decodes during a timed run. The
  viewer uses three.js 0.147 UMD + OrbitControls from jsDelivr, one renderer with scissor viewports and one shared
  camera. Meshes are decimated with VTK to ≈ 20k faces (largest component and strays decimated separately so
  fragments survive) and stored as uint16-quantized base64 in `report/meshes/<id>.js`. playwright (pip only,
  driving the installed Chrome) runs the headless viewer check.
